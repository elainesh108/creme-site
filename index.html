CREME

Calibrating Decision Robustness via
Inverse Conformal Risk Control

Robust optimization needs a robustness level (a radius, a confidence, a coverage target), and that number is usually picked by hand. CREME inverts the logic of conformal risk control: instead of fixing a risk target and solving for a set, it takes the uncertainty set as given and certifies, with finite-sample guarantees, the miscoverage and regret it actually buys you. Sweep the parameter and the trade-off traces itself.

Miscoverage – regret frontier

Conformal risk control, run backwards

Standard CRC

Pick a risk level first, say “5% miscoverage”, then search for a set or decision rule that meets it. The target is fixed; the set is solved for.

⇄

Inverse CRC (this paper)

Fix the uncertainty set, that is, the robustness level \(\lambda\) an engineer already uses, and certify the miscoverage and regret it actually incurs. The set is given; the risk is solved for.

Sweeping \(\lambda\) traces a curve, not a point: as the uncertainty set shrinks, miscoverage rises and regret falls, and vice versa. CREME's estimators \(\hat\alpha_I(\lambda)\) and \(\hat\alpha_R(\lambda)\) stay valid at every \(\lambda\) simultaneously, so the whole curve, a certified Pareto frontier, can be handed to a decision-maker instead of a single ad hoc number.

Method

From a fixed \(\lambda\) to a certified frontier

The full derivation is in the paper; here is the chain of definitions the demo below runs on live.

Notation
\(X\in\mathcal X,\ Y\in\mathcal Y\)context and outcome, revealed after the decision
\(z\in\mathcal Z\)decision variable; \(f(y,z)\) its cost under outcome \(y\)
\(\lambda\in\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb R_+\)robustness level; nested sets \(\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\Rightarrow\mathcal U_{\lambda_1}\subseteq\mathcal U_{\lambda_2}\)
\(\mathcal U_\lambda(X)\)uncertainty set for \(Y\) at level \(\lambda\)
\(z^*_\lambda(X)\)robust decision at level \(\lambda\)
\(I_\lambda,\ R_\lambda\)miscoverage loss (0/1) and regret loss (\(\ge 0\))
\(\hat\alpha_I(\lambda),\ \hat\alpha_R(\lambda)\)certified upper bounds on expected miscoverage / regret
\(n,\ B,\ \delta\)calibration size, loss bound, failure probability
\(\hat\lambda\)robustness level chosen post hoc from the frontier

1. The robust decision

For nested sets \(\mathcal U_\lambda(X)\), the robust decision minimizes the worst case inside the set:

\[ z^*_\lambda(X) := \argmin_{z\in\mathcal Z}\ \max_{y\in\mathcal U_\lambda(X)} f(y,z) \]

Its two failure modes are scored per observation:

\[ I_\lambda(X,Y) := \mathbb 1[\,Y\notin\mathcal U_\lambda(X)\,],\qquad R_\lambda(X,Y) := f(Y,z^*_\lambda(X)) - \min_{z\in\mathcal Z} f(Y,z) \]

2. What "certified" means

An estimator is certified when it upper-bounds the true expected loss at every \(\lambda\) at once:

\[ \hat\alpha_I(\lambda) \ge \mathbb E[I_\lambda(X,Y)],\qquad \hat\alpha_R(\lambda) \ge \mathbb E[R_\lambda(X,Y)] \]

3. The conformalized risk estimator

Given a calibration average \(\bar\ell_n(\lambda)=\tfrac1n\sum_i \ell_\lambda(x_i,y_i)\) and loss bound \(B\), the estimator inflates the empirical average by a worst-case correction for the unseen point:

\[ \tilde\alpha_\ell(\lambda) = \frac{n}{n+1}\,\bar\ell_n(\lambda) + \frac{B}{n+1} \]

This is the number the demo plots. No assumption on the distribution of \(Y\) is needed, only exchangeability and a bounded loss.

4. Validity, with a finite-sample rate

\[ \mathbb E[\hat\alpha_\ell(\lambda)] \ge \mathbb E[\ell_\lambda(X_{n+1},Y_{n+1})] \]

and with probability at least \(1-\delta\) over an i.i.d. draw,

\[ \big|\hat\alpha_\ell(\lambda) - \mathbb E[\ell_\lambda(X_{n+1},Y_{n+1})]\big| \le \frac{B}{n+1}\left(\sqrt{\tfrac n2 \log\tfrac2\delta} + 2 + \tfrac1B\right) =: \epsilon \]

5. Why the curve is a frontier

Under a mild monotonicity condition (shrinking \(\mathcal U_\lambda\) cannot raise the conditional expected cost), the true risk pair is weakly decreasing in \(\lambda\):

\[ \alpha_I(\lambda_1)\ge\alpha_I(\lambda_2),\qquad \alpha_R(\lambda_1)\le\alpha_R(\lambda_2)\quad(\lambda_1\le\lambda_2) \]

so its image is exactly the Pareto frontier of the robust policy family. Because \(\hat\alpha_I,\hat\alpha_R\) upper-bound the true values pointwise, the pruned lower-left envelope of \(\{(\hat\alpha_I(\lambda),\hat\alpha_R(\lambda))\}\) is a certified (conservative but valid) outer approximation of that frontier.

6. Choosing \(\lambda\) after looking

A decision-maker who picks \(\hat\lambda=\argmax_\lambda g(\hat\alpha_I(\lambda),\hat\alpha_R(\lambda))\) after seeing the frontier breaks exchangeability, which can erode validity by a selection bias term \(\Delta(g,\mathcal P)\). CREME restores it with a single split: the frontier shown for selection is built from one half of the calibration data, and \(\hat\lambda\) is re-scored on the untouched other half, which restores an exact finite-sample guarantee at the selected point.

Algorithm 1 · CREME

  1. 1Split calibration data \(\mathcal D\) into \(\mathcal D_1,\mathcal D_2\).
  2. 2For each \(\lambda\) on the grid, compute \(\hat\alpha_I(\lambda),\hat\alpha_R(\lambda)\) from \(\mathcal D_1\) via step 3's estimator.
  3. 3Prune dominated points to form the pre-hoc frontier \(\hat{\mathcal F}^{(1)}\).
  4. 4A decision-maker selects \(\hat\lambda\) from \(\hat{\mathcal F}^{(1)}\).
  5. 5Recalibrate on \(\mathcal D_2\): report the post-hoc valid \(\big(\hat\alpha_I^{(2)}(\hat\lambda),\hat\alpha_R^{(2)}(\hat\lambda)\big)\).

From the paper

What certification buys you

100%

validity across all four optimization paradigms tested, versus ≤50% for the naive Monte Carlo average at matched sample sizes.

\(O(n^{-1/2})\)

the estimation error decays at a parametric rate: accuracy improves fast even though the guarantee never relies on knowing the outcome distribution.

4

optimization paradigms benchmarked: linear programming, the newsvendor problem, portfolio selection, and shortest path.
